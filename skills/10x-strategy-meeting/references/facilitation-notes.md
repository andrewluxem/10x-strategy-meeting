# Facilitation notes

Depth for running the session. Read when building a packet for a first-time facilitator, or when the user asks why a rule exists.

## The three roles

Every group of 3 carries all three roles. With more than 3 per group, double the Idea Generators, never the Challengers.

- **Idea Generator.** Proposes bold ideas. The job is volume and reach, not polish. A generator who self-censors for feasibility is doing the Challenger's job at the wrong time.
- **Challenger.** Questions ideas to explore feasibility and scale, never to dismiss. The Challenger's move is "what would have to be true," not "that will not work." Challengers stay quiet during generation and work during the challenge block.
- **Note Taker.** Documents every idea and the discussion around it. An undocumented idea did not happen. The Note Taker captures the why, not just the what, because the why is what survives into goals.

## Why-not-how

The session asks "Why should we get there," never "How do we get there." How-thinking collapses the idea space to what the team already knows how to build, which is exactly the 10 percent path the session exists to escape. The how gets its turn later, when captured ideas are split into team goals and planned.

The practical rule for the room: feasibility objections are out of order during generation. The facilitator redirects them to the challenge block.

## Removing prior assumptions

10x thinking usually requires going back to the first concept of the idea and dropping an assumption the business has been carrying. The facilitator should name one droppable assumption in the framing, as permission. Working from an unconstrained future backward is the mechanism; imagine the end state first, then reason back to what it requires.

## The framing contrast

The framing block earns its minutes. Ten percent improvement takes the path everyone else is already on. An order of magnitude forces reimagining what can be done. A concrete contrast in the business's own terms lands better than the abstract principle: name what a 10 percent version of the business looks like, then what a tenfold version would have to be.

## Present-back

Groups present the what and the why. Any medium works: text, pictures, diagrams, role play. The medium rule exists to keep polish from becoming a filter; a sketch presented with conviction beats a deck.

## Failure modes

- **10 percent creep.** Ideas that optimize the existing funnel arrive dressed as moonshots. Flag them in capture rather than debating them in the room; they may be good ideas that belong in a different meeting.
- **Feasibility gating during generation.** The most senior person asking "how would that work" ends ideation early. The facilitator owns this interruption regardless of who makes it.
- **No Note Taker.** The session produces energy and nothing else. If a group has no Note Taker, the packet says so before the session starts.
- **Ideas without routing.** A capture document with no routing table is a brainstorm, not a strategy session. Every idea gets a next step, an owner, and a date, or a visible placeholder where one is missing.

## After the session

Captured ideas become goals the whole team or organization can direct effort toward. Parts of an idea can split into specific team goals with the intent of reaching the 10x outcome over the horizon. That conversion is the business-goals skill's job; the capture document is built to hand off cleanly.
